NOTE · 21d
The Inactive screen. Same 40pt band the whole app uses to get out of a full-screen surface — not a navigation push, which this shell has none of. No explanatory paragraph, no inline link. Close all sits in the band as a real control. Each card's kind footer earns its keep by carrying the age: NOTE · 24d. No + and no Done here.
Why it differs from Chrome.

The word. Inactive, literally — the owner's rule: names say what they describe. (The original Liv called it "parking" in its tab menu, liv-ui-map.md:3984; Chrome's word wins because it is the plainer one.)

No sentences. Chrome explains itself twice: a subtitle under the row and a paragraph on the screen, with a blue "settings" link. All three are refused by a standing ruling — say it with the app's own chips, or don't say it.

No push. This shell has zero NavigationStacks. A screen reached from a full-screen surface wears the 40pt band, like every feature window.

No confirm, no undo. Closing a tab writes nothing to the box — the note is still there, in search, in Everything, in its workspace. The undo chip is reserved for real box writes.

The age is on the card. Chrome shows no per-card age; Liv already has a kind footer with room in it, and "why is this here" is then answerable without a sentence.
